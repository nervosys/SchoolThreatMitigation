# Washington K–12 School Safety — Video Briefing

Version 14.5 advisory summary. Independent research, courtesy of NERVOSYS, LLC. Not government-endorsed.

## 00 — SAFER SCHOOLS. PROVEN READINESS.
School safety is a system of prevention, warning, immediate protection, and lifesaving care. These are the priority recommendations from the Washington K through twelve advisory.

## 01 — THE FIRST MINUTES MATTER
Plan for the first minutes without assuming police can arrive within five minutes. Every occupied setting must receive clear instructions and be able to act.

## 02 — CLOSE THE BASIC GAPS
Close basic life-safety gaps first. Verify required emergency planning, accessible drills, responder coordination, and Washington's bleeding-control equipment, staff training, and inspections.

## 03 — PREVENT. THEN VERIFY.
Prevent harm through multidisciplinary behavioral assessment and trusted reporting. Address violence and cyberbullying, but independently verify allegations and synthetic evidence. Protect due process.

## 04 — TEST WHAT ACTUALLY WORKS
Test the system where students actually are: classrooms, portable buildings, school buses, and outdoor programs. Measure alert reach, protective decisions, accessible egress, and medical handoffs.

## 05 — TECHNOLOGY IS CONDITIONAL
Use advanced security tools only to close a documented gap. Screening, artificial intelligence cameras, unmanned-system detection, canines, and protective personnel require lawful approval, qualified supervision, and independent testing.

## 06 — LEAD LOCAL IMPROVEMENT
Give each campus leader a named owner, resources, and permission to propose safe improvements. Run a ninety-day assurance program. Track failures as carefully as successes.

## 07 — EVIDENCE OVER ASSUMPTIONS
Apply a clear evidence ladder: documented, inspected, exercised, then independently verified. A failed warning, blocked accessible exit, or missing mandated control cannot be hidden by a good average score.

## 08 — INNOVATE NATIONWIDE
Create a national, school-led innovation forum with optional federal technical advice. Share measured outcomes, costs, failures, and replicable practices, while protecting students and sensitive security information.

## 09 — AUTHORIZE. TEST. VERIFY.
District boards should fund ninety-day campus reviews, close critical gaps, and require retesting. Until a campus is assessed, readiness is unverified. This advisory is independent research, not government endorsement.
